These lessons apply to almost any game, but they've proven especially useful with incremental games, where small teams and short development cycles are common.

## Choosing what to make

- **Look at what players want right now.** Your own interests matter, but if you hope to make a living from your games, pay close attention to what players are looking for today.
- **Trends can age well.** Memes and trending topics can inspire games that don't necessarily feel dated later. [How Many Dudes?](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3934270/How_Many_Dudes/), which asks how many dudes it takes to beat a gorilla, is a good example.
- **Follow the 80/20 rule.** Build about 80% of your game on a formula that has been established and polished over the years, and make the remaining 20% your own. That 20% is what sets your game apart. The 80% is what makes it feel good to play from the start.

## Design

- **Offer a meaningful choice early.** Let players unlock an item or buy an upgrade as soon as possible, so they feel their decisions matter from the first minutes.
- **Thank the player at the end.** A warm, heartfelt thank-you when the game ends leaves a lasting impression, and players remember how a game made them feel.

## Production

- **Plan localization from day one.** Supporting multiple languages is much easier when it's part of the plan from the start of development, rather than something you retrofit before launch.

## Launch

- **Aim for your first 10 positive reviews quickly.** Steam only shows a review score once a game has at least 10 user reviews, and developers widely report that reaching that mark early noticeably improves a game's visibility. Plan how you'll get those first players, such as friends, communities or content creators, before launch day.
