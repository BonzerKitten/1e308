Progression is the heart of an incremental game. The feeling of always moving toward something bigger is the genre's main appeal, so it deserves more design attention than anything else.

## Give players a skill tree

A skill tree full of upgrades is one of the genre's must-haves. It shows players where they can go next, gives them something to plan around, and turns every purchase into a small decision rather than a formality.

## Add a prestige system

A prestige system, often called ascension or rebirth, asks players to give up some of their resources in exchange for unlocking new parts of the game. In some games, players also give up other parts of their progression, such as upgrades they've already bought.

Done well, prestige turns a slowdown into a fresh start. The player goes back to the beginning, but with something new to discover and a reason to keep going.

## Don't require every upgrade

Players should be able to finish the game without buying every single upgrade.

If reaching the end means buying everything, the choices stop mattering. Players end up working through a checklist instead of making progress, and they lose the sense of progression that drew them to the genre in the first place.

:::tip
Give players a meaningful choice as early as possible, such as unlocking an item or picking their first upgrade. It tells them right away that their decisions shape the game.
:::
